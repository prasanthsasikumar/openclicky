# Accessibility and HCI prior art: LLM desktop assistants for older adults, BLV users and novices, with emphasis on on-device / OS-vendor models

Research date: 2026-10-27. Scope: papers 2023–2026 (plus two seminal earlier items), products 2023–2026. Peer-reviewed vs. preprint and shipped vs. announced are marked throughout. "Not independently verified" means a single source, usually secondary.

## Key question 1: Which published papers evaluate an LLM-based desktop/computer-use assistant with older adults or BLV users, and do any run the model on-device or argue for it?

### Takeaway
A small but fast-growing set of peer-reviewed studies (ASSETS 2024, CHI 2026 x3, IUI 2026, EMNLP 2026, ASSETS 2025/2026) evaluates LLM/VLM assistants for computer and smartphone tasks with blind users and older adults; every one of them that names its model uses a cloud frontier model (GPT-4/GPT-5, Operator, Gemini). I found no peer-reviewed paper that evaluated an on-device LLM desktop assistant with these populations; the on-device argument appears only in product engineering posts (Google TalkBack) and one smart-glasses journal paper.

### Cited Findings

**Desktop / computer-use agents with blind users**
- Savant (ASSETS 2024, peer-reviewed): "Enabling Uniform Computer Interaction Experience for Blind Users through Large Language Models", Kodandaram, Uckun, Bi, Ramakrishnan, Ashok (Stony Brook / Old Dominion), DOI 10.1145/3663548.3675605. LLM tool that lets screen-reader users drive any application via natural-language commands, automating sequences of screen-reader actions on controls; user study with 11 blind participants showed significant gains in efficiency and usability over current practice — [arXiv 2407.19537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19537); [Stony Brook record](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/enabling-uniform-computer-interaction-experience-for-blind-users-/). Jakob Nielsen's summary of the same group's work reports the 2024 study's agent success rate as 27% across Excel, Outlook, File Explorer and three other apps — [Nielsen UX Roundup 2026-09-25](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ux-roundup-20260925) (secondary).
- OLLA / "Are We There Yet? Assessing Computer-Use Agents for Blind Users' Accessible Interaction with Desktop Applications", Kodandaram, Padma Reddy, Bi, Zhou, Ramakrishnan, Ashok; arXiv 2609.00524 submitted 1 Sep 2026; "Accepted to the EMNLP 2026 Main Conference". Three-week diary/field study, 8 blind screen-reader users on their own PCs, 1,258 commands across 12 applications (mainly Word, Excel, PowerPoint); the prototype reads the Microsoft UI Automation tree. GPT-5 achieved the highest success rate at 52.5%; four other models scored 38–49%; failure classes: grounding, planning, constraint-tracking, termination; interviews surfaced "beyond-automation" needs — [arXiv 2609.00524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00524). Nielsen's write-up adds: 34% partial completions averaging 68% of steps, 13% outright failures; failure examples include hallucinated controls, options hidden behind unopened dialogs, and an export dialog navigated correctly but Save never clicked; participants wanted the agent to "tell me where I am, what options are available, and what I should do next", wanted confirmation before hard-to-undo actions and a reliable "done" signal; some avoided banking/passwords and stopped delegating task types that kept failing — [Nielsen UX Roundup 2026-09-25](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ux-roundup-20260925). Model was cloud (GPT-5); no on-device discussion on the abstract page.
- "Toward Independent Online Shopping of the Visually Impaired Through Voice-based Computer-Using Agent", Shin, Oh, Kim, Eom, Lee (Yonsei), CHI 2026 (peer-reviewed), DOI 10.1145/3772318.3791681, article 160, published 13 Apr 2026. Semi-automatic Wizard-of-Oz study: 12 Korean adults (totally blind or severe low vision, all acquired) did a real Naver Shopping task using OpenAI's Operator CUA with a human wizard transcribing speech to bypass STT errors; qualitative — participants compensated for missing visual/social cues by seeking trends, aesthetic impressions, colour descriptions and social proof — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791681); [a11ybob summary](https://a11ybob.com/writing/reviews/69e63e5f5570d11947cedf8f) (secondary).
- "'Zooming In' on Agentic Web Browsers as Assistive Technologies: A Case Study with a Low-Vision Technology Expert", Colazzo & Anzillotti, arXiv 2606.24870 (preprint, 23 Jun 2026). Single-participant case study; reports that "despite the current limitations, the navigation experience is notably fluid and flexible" — [arXiv 2606.24870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24870).
- "Mapping the Design Space of User Experience for Computer Use Agents", Cheng, Liang (CMU), Schoop, Nichols (Apple), IUI 2026 (peer-reviewed), arXiv 2602.07283. Review of nine computer-use agents announced 2024–2025, interviews with eight UX/AI practitioners, then a 20-participant Wizard-of-Oz study with normal, error-prone and risky task execution; taxonomy covers user prompts, explainability of agent activity, user control, and users' mental models — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/mapping); [arXiv 2602.07283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07283v1). Not population-specific, but it is the Apple-authored design-space paper for exactly this interaction class.

**Mobile LLM assistants with blind users**
- Insight (arXiv 2605.09803, preprint, 10 May 2026), Ansah et al.: Android accessibility service with natural-language interaction and real-time screen summarisation compared within-subjects to TalkBack; reports reduced mental effort and task time, preference for dialogue, and a need for better interruption management; sample size and model not stated on the abstract page — [arXiv 2605.09803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09803). A search summary quoted the paper as choosing Gemini's API for latency, i.e. cloud — [search summary of 2605.09803](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09803v1) (not independently verified).
- "How Multimodal Large Language Models Support Access to Visual Information: A Diary Study With Blind and Low Vision People", Gonzalez Penuela, Jung, Lin, Hu, Azenkot; arXiv 2602.13469, "to appear" CHI 2026. 20 BLV participants, two-week diary with an MLLM visual-interpretation app: trustworthiness 3.76/5, satisfaction 4.13/5, 22.2% incorrect answers, 10.8% abstentions; proposes a "visual assistant" skill with guidelines for goal-directed, reliable assistance — [arXiv 2602.13469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13469).
- "(Computer) Vision in Action: Comparing Remote Sighted Assistance and a Multimodal Voice Agent in Inspection Sequences", Rudaz, Nino Carreras, Merlino, Due, Brown; arXiv 2602.05671, "Conditionally accepted at CHI 2026". Granular analysis of a blind participant doing the same task with a human remote sighted assistant and with a multimodal voice agent; concludes that until agents can produce "environmentally occasioned vision-based actions" they lack a key proactive resource humans use — [arXiv 2602.05671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05671).

**Older adults and novices**
- GuideMe (CHI 2026, peer-reviewed): "GuideMe: A VLM-Based System Assisting Independent Smartphone Learning for Older Adults", Fang, Zhang, Ni, Hui, Wang, DOI 10.1145/3772318.3791448. Formative study with 16 Chinese older adults (55–86) using interviews, contextual observation across six apps and eye-tracking; found effective in-person help = describe the problem, confirm intent with counter-questions, point at the element. System: Android, Media Projection screenshots + Accessibility Service UI tree, GPT-5 in the cloud, asks clarifying questions, then shows a semi-transparent overlay that "hollows out" the target as a machine pointing gesture. Evaluation N=18 (mean age 63.6), within-subjects vs LLM-powered Baidu search vs confederate in-person tutor: completion time 56.5 s vs 82.9 s vs 48.7 s; UI search time 1.26 s vs 4.23 s vs 0.81 s; incorrect clicks 0.11 vs 2.94 vs 0.55; NASA-TLX mental demand 1.72 vs 5.33 vs 1.44; asking time longer with GuideMe (26.3 s vs 18.3 s) because of the clarification loop; a minority raised privacy concerns about screen reading and the cloud VLM question was "acknowledged but not resolved" — [a11y-paradise review of GuideMe](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69e7deb19cd3fefd1e4d88bd) (secondary; ACM page returned 403); [ACM DL entry](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791448).
- EasyAsk (IMWUT vol. 8 no. 3, 2024, peer-reviewed): Gao & Du (Tsinghua), DOI 10.1145/3678516. In-app contextual tutorial search for older adults with voice + touch; two LLMs (one interprets voice + context, one searches tutorials); 18 older participants; correct tutorial in 98.94% of cases — [Tsinghua PDF](https://pi.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/3678516.pdf).
- ElderBench (arXiv 2609.04850, preprint, 4 Sep 2026), Zhan et al.: 249 naturally elicited smartphone tasks across 20 apps; older adults' instructions differ syntactically, semantically and pragmatically from benchmark instructions (indirect speech, referential ambiguity, under-specification) and mainstream GUI agents/VLMs show "substantial performance degradation" on them — [arXiv 2609.04850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04850).
- "Helping the Helper: LLM-Assisted Problem Articulation for Older Adults Seeking Technology Support", Sharifi, Shomee, Lamar, Medya, Chattopadhyay; arXiv 2601.10018 (preprint, v2 26 Aug 2026). Diary study with 27 older adults found four barriers (verbosity, incompleteness, over-, under-specification); LLM rephrasing raised automated solution accuracy from 35% to 69%; 48 younger helpers understood rephrased queries 93.7% vs 65.8%; 34 older adults rated solutions 94.7% actionable — [arXiv 2601.10018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10018).
- "Exploring the Role of LLMs for Supporting Older Adults: Opportunities and Concerns", Kaliappan, Anand, Saha, Karkar; CHI 2024 workshop short paper (HCI and Aging); position piece flagging privacy, security and accuracy concerns — [arXiv 2411.08123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08123).
- "Balancing Safety and Autonomy: Accessibility-Oriented Interventions in Generative AI for Cognitive Impairment", Meng, Chen, Ye, Liu, Lu; accepted ASSETS 2026, arXiv 2608.17175. Qualitative study with 45 people with cognitive impairment and caregivers; five mechanisms: AI Capability Constraint, Human Oversight Embedding, Cognitive Engagement Maintenance, Human-AI Relationship Regulation, Risk Transparency and Control; protective mechanisms help severe impairment but restrict autonomy at mild impairment — [arXiv 2608.17175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17175).
- Low-literacy voice-first (India): a 2025 ACL HCI+NLP workshop paper compared the form-based myScheme app with a voice-first generative-AI prototype ("Prabodhini") for welfare schemes with 15 low-income participants and argues voice-first fits low-literacy mental models — [ACL Anthology listing](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.hcinlp-1.6) (page returned 404 on fetch; details from search summary, not independently verified). FormBharo (arXiv 2608.06027, 2026) is an LLM voice agent for form filling piloted with low-income Hindi-speaking mothers in rural Maharashtra — [arXiv 2608.06027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06027).

**On-device argument in the literature**
- The only on-device-specific accessibility paper found is a 2026 Electronics journal article on privacy-preserving smart-glasses navigation that fine-tunes a smaller VLM to run on glasses + phone "without an external server", arguing that network instability is "particularly severe for blind users" — [MDPI Electronics 15(14):3223](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15143223). It is not a desktop assistant.
- "Towards Blind and Low-Vision Accessibility of Lightweight VLMs and Custom LLM-Evals" (arXiv 2511.10615) exists but I only saw the listing — [arXiv 2511.10615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10615).
- Seminal on-device precedent: Apple's "Screen Recognition: Creating Accessibility Metadata for Mobile Applications from Pixels" (CHI 2021 Best Paper, ~78,000 screenshots from >4,000 apps) shipped in iOS 14 and runs on-device — [CMU news](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/news/2021/cmu-apple-team-improves-ios-app-accessibility); [AFB AccessWorld](https://afb.org/aw/spring2024/apple-screen-recognition-machine-learning-accessibility). Apple's AXNav (CHI 2024, oral) replays accessibility tests from natural language — [Apple at CHI 2024](https://machinelearning.apple.com/updates/apple-at-chi-2024); [arXiv 2310.02424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02424).

### Inferences
- Sample sizes cluster at 8–20 (OLLA 8, Savant 11, Shin 12, GuideMe 18, EasyAsk 18, Azenkot diary 20); a 10–20 participant study with 2–3 week in-the-wild use is the field norm and would be publishable at ASSETS/CHI.
- Every evaluated system is cloud-hosted; the privacy question is raised by participants (GuideMe) and position papers (Kaliappan; Chen et al. below) but has not been answered by building an on-device alternative and testing it with the population. That is an open slot.
- The OLLA results (52.5% full success with GPT-5) suggest that even frontier cloud models fail roughly half of self-chosen office tasks for blind users; a small on-device model will not do better at autonomous execution, which supports a guidance-first design rather than an automation-first one.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found of an LLM desktop assistant evaluated with older adults specifically (GuideMe, EasyAsk, ElderBench are smartphone); desktop + older adults is uncovered.
- No paper found that runs an on-device LLM/VLM for a desktop assistant with BLV or older users.
- Could not access the full text of GuideMe, OLLA or the IUI 2026 design-space paper; numbers above come from abstracts and one secondary review each.
- No ASSETS 2025 or UIST 2025 paper on LLM desktop agents for blind users surfaced in searches; may exist under a title I did not hit.

## Key question 2: Which shipped accessibility features use the OS's own on-device model today, and which fall back to the cloud?

### Takeaway
Three OS vendors ship on-device model-backed accessibility features: Google (TalkBack image descriptions on Gemini Nano since Sept 2024, with server Gemini as opt-in fallback), Microsoft (Narrator rich image descriptions on Copilot+ PCs since April 2025 with a locally downloaded model; Fluid Dictation on Phi Silica since Sept 2025; a Copilot cloud path for non-Copilot+ PCs since Jan 2026), and Apple (Screen Recognition/VoiceOver on-device since 2020, Live Recognition on visionOS, Personal Voice; but Apple has not stated that any accessibility feature uses the Apple Intelligence foundation model). Chrome ships Gemini Nano APIs on desktop but with heavy hardware floors and no accessibility feature built on them.

### Cited Findings

**Google / Android / Chrome**
- TalkBack + Gemini Nano (shipped, Sept 2024): when users opt in on eligible devices, TalkBack uses Gemini Nano's multimodal model to automatically describe images without alt text in apps such as Google Photos and Chrome, "even when offline or on a weak connection"; Google's team said an on-device model was "the only feasible solution" for automatic detailed descriptions and it replaced the earlier small model Garcon that produced short generic output; Gemini Nano accepts 512 px or 768 px images and the team chose richer output at the cost of latency, streaming text to TTS; for more detail users open the TalkBack menu and choose "Describe Image", which sends the image to server-side Gemini 1.5 Flash — [Android Developers Blog, Sept 2024](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2024/09/talkback-uses-gemini-nano-to-increase-low-vision-accessibility.html).
- TalkBack Gemini Q&A (announced 15 May 2025, GAAD): users can ask Gemini questions about images and about "their whole screen" (e.g., material of an item, whether a discount applies); the post does not say whether this is on-device or cloud; same post: Expressive Captions (Android 15+, English, US/UK/CA/AU), Chrome desktop OCR for scanned PDFs so screen readers work on them, Page Zoom on Chrome Android, Chromebook Face Control and Reading Mode — [Google Keyword blog, 2025-05-15](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/accessibility/android-gemini-ai-gaad-2025/).
- Chrome built-in AI (shipped in stages): Prompt, Summarizer, Writer, Rewriter, Proofreader, Translator and Language Detector APIs run "with Gemini Nano in Chrome"; requirements: Windows 10/11, macOS 13+, Linux or ChromeOS on Chromebook Plus; 22 GB free disk (model removed if free space drops below 10 GB), GPU with >4 GB VRAM, 16 GB RAM, 4+ cores; from Chrome 149 the models handle English, Spanish, Japanese, German, French; the docs contain no accessibility guidance — [developer.chrome.com built-in AI](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/built-in); [get started](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/get-started). I found no official Chrome "Help me read" feature; Chromebook Reading Mode coverage does not mention AI.

**Microsoft / Windows**
- Narrator rich image descriptions (shipped to Insiders April 2025 on Snapdragon Copilot+ PCs; AMD/Intel Copilot+ in July 2025): Narrator key + Ctrl + D gives a contextual description covering "people, objects, colors, text, and numbers"; required models download on first use; non-Copilot+ PCs "will continue to give you very basic image descriptions" — [Windows Insider blog, build 26200.5710, 2025-07-18](https://blogs.windows.com/windows-insider/2025/07/18/announcing-windows-11-insider-preview-build-26200-5710-dev-channel/); April 2025 Snapdragon-first note in build 26200.5551 thread — [ElevenForum KB5055617](https://www.elevenforum.com/t/kb5055617-windows-11-insider-dev-build-26200-5551-24h2-april-11.35124/latest) (secondary). The same July build post says Click to Do's "describe image" action processes images, charts and graphs "locally on your device" and that the Privacy & security "Text and image generation" page will show third-party apps using Windows-provided generative models.
- Narrator + Copilot (Insider build 26220.7535, 9 Jan 2026): on all Windows 11 devices except the EEA, Narrator key + Ctrl + D describes the focused image and Narrator key + Ctrl + S the full screen via Copilot with follow-up prompts; "On Copilot+ PCs, Narrator continues to provide instant, on-device image descriptions using local AI"; the image is shared only after the user chooses to describe it — [Windows Insider blog 2026-01-09](https://blogs.windows.com/windows-insider/2026/01/09/announcing-windows-11-insider-preview-build-26220-7535-dev-beta-channels); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/microsoft-brings-another-ai-feature-to-all-windows-11-devices-without-special-hardware/). Release Preview builds 26100.8106 / 26200.8106 list it, suggesting movement to GA; no GA date confirmed.
- Fluid Dictation in Voice Access (Insider build 26220.5790, early Sept 2025; Copilot+ PCs, all English locales): corrects grammar, punctuation and filler words as you speak, "powered by on-device small language models", model downloads in the background when Voice Access launches, disabled in password/PIN fields, toggled by voice — [Microsoft Support: Fluid dictation](https://support.microsoft.com/ja-jp/Accessibility/windows/voice-access/fluid-dictation); [Windows Report](https://windowsreport.com/?p=1469493). Phi Silica is "based on a Cyber-EO compliant derivative of Phi-3.5-mini" for NPU Copilot+ PCs — [Neowin on Phi Silica](https://www.neowin.net/news/microsoft-reveals-the-phi-silica-ai-small-language-model-for-npu-based-copilot-pcs/).

**Apple**
- Screen Recognition / VoiceOver image descriptions: on-device since iOS 14 (2020), see Q1 seminal entry — [AFB AccessWorld](https://afb.org/aw/spring2024/apple-screen-recognition-machine-learning-accessibility); [Perkins](https://www.perkins.org/resource/ios-14-screen-recognition/).
- Apple's 13 May 2025 announcement (features shipped with the 2025 OS releases; macOS 26 Tahoe released 15 Sept 2025 per AppleVis): Accessibility Reader (systemwide reading mode on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Vision Pro, with Spoken Content, built into Magnifier); Magnifier for Mac (camera/Continuity Camera, Desk View); Braille Access; Live Recognition on visionOS "uses on-device machine learning to describe surroundings, find objects, read documents"; Personal Voice now from 10 phrases "leveraging advances in on-device machine learning"; Assistive Access adds an Apple TV player and a developer API; Vehicle Motion Cues on Mac; Voice Control programming mode in Xcode. The release does not mention Apple Intelligence at all and makes no on-device claim for Accessibility Reader, Magnifier or VoiceOver — [Apple Newsroom 2025-05-13](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/05/apple-unveils-powerful-accessibility-features-coming-later-this-year/); [AppleVis macOS Tahoe](https://www.applevis.com/blog/macos-tahoe-new-features-changes-improvements-bugs-blind-deafblind-low-vision-users); [Apple Mac User Guide: Accessibility Reader](https://support.apple.com/en-mo/guide/mac-help/apd07d671600). Note: Live Recognition is a visionOS feature, not macOS, in this release.
- Apple Intelligence generally routes most requests on-device and sends "more complex requests" to Private Cloud Compute; Writing Tools (proofread/rewrite) are part of Apple Intelligence; no Apple source found that documents which Writing Tools requests leave the device — [Wikipedia: Apple Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Intelligence) (secondary).
- Foundation Models framework (shipped with iOS/macOS 26, Sept 2025): developers call the on-device model from Swift; Apple's examples are quizzes and workout summaries, none accessibility-specific — [Apple Newsroom 2025-09](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/09/apples-foundation-models-framework-unlocks-new-intelligent-app-experiences/).

Cloud.

**Clicky / HeyClicky / OpenClicky (cursor-side desktop assistants)**
- Clicky (farzaa, MIT): macOS 14.2+ menu-bar app that "lives as a buddy next to your cursor", sees the screen, talks, and points; Claude for chat with screenshot, AssemblyAI real-time STT, ElevenLabs TTS, all via a Cloudflare Worker proxy; pointing via `[POINT:x,y:label:screenN]` tags rendered by a transparent overlay that moves the cursor; permissions: Microphone, Accessibility (global ⌃⌥ shortcut), Screen Recording, Screen Content; README targets "those that want to hack on it" and does not mention older adults or accessibility; later development is private at HeyClicky — [farzaa/clicky README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/farzaa/clicky/main/README.md).
- HeyClicky (commercial, YC Spring 2026 per directories): "an ai buddy that lives on your computer"; sees the screen only on hotkey; teaches any tool and walks you through what you are stuck on; "heyclicky agent" executes tasks; draws on screen to show where to go; macOS 14.2+ and Windows 10+; Free (25 talk + 25 agent messages/month), Pro $20, Max $100; "same frontier models everyone else is using"; screenshots never stored, text summaries kept; no mention of older adults or accessibility, though it aims at "the next billion people" — [heyclicky.com](https://www.heyclicky.com/).
- OpenClicky (the user's project; site fetched for completeness): "a free, open-source voice assistant for Mac and Windows"; dictation uses Apple's on-device recognizer by default and works offline; own OpenAI/Anthropic keys go direct to the vendor; pointer flies from the notch to the answer on ⌃⌥; "Teach Me" draws labelled diagrams; simple tasks run immediately, larger jobs go to a Codex agent; MIT; the site does not mention older adults or low vision — [openclicky.flowsxr.com](https://openclicky.flowsxr.com/).
- Voice control: Talon and Dragon remain command-and-dictation tools; I found no evidence of LLM or on-device LLM integration in either — [Spokenly comparison](https://spokenly.app/comparison/talon-voice) (vendor page, low reliability).

### Inferences
- The pattern across all three OS vendors is identical: a small on-device model handles the always-on, high-frequency, low-stakes job (automatic image captions, dictation clean-up), and a cloud model is an explicit, user-initiated escalation ("Describe Image" in TalkBack; Copilot in Narrator; "More info" in Seeing AI). This two-tier design is the de-facto standard and a direct template for an OS-model-first desktop assistant.
- NV Access's revert of local captioning is the clearest evidence of the cost side: a 2022-era ViT-GPT2 caption model was judged not good enough to ship, and runtime weight (numpy/onnxruntime, ARM64 emulation) blocked it. Vendor-managed models (Gemini Nano, Phi Silica, Apple Foundation Models) remove that packaging burden, which is the strongest practical argument for using the OS model rather than bundling one.
- Nothing shipped by Apple ties the Apple Intelligence foundation model to VoiceOver, Accessibility Reader or Assistive Access; Apple's on-device accessibility ML is task-specific (Screen Recognition, Live Recognition, Personal Voice). An Apple-model-based accessibility assistant would therefore be novel on the platform.

### Gaps
- No official source for whether TalkBack's May 2025 "ask about the whole screen" runs on Gemini Nano or in the cloud.
- GA dates in the stable Windows channel for Narrator rich descriptions and Narrator+Copilot were not confirmed; sources are Insider posts.
- No Apple documentation found on which Writing Tools operations stay on-device vs Private Cloud Compute, nor whether VoiceOver uses any Apple Intelligence model.
- GNOME Orca: no source found on any AI/LLM description work in 2025–2026.
- Chrome "Help me read": no official feature by that name found.

## Key question 3: Has anyone built an assistant on Apple Intelligence / Foundation Models or Phi Silica for a low-literacy or older-adult audience? Windows or Linux equivalents?

### Takeaway
No. I found no product, paper or open-source project that builds an older-adult or low-literacy assistant on Apple's Foundation Models framework, on Phi Silica/Windows AI APIs, or on Gemini Nano in Chrome; the only third-party local-model accessibility tooling is NVDA add-ons wiring Ollama/LiteRT-LM/llama.cpp, aimed at blind power users.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's Foundation Models framework launch post lists quizzes and workout summaries as example uses; nothing accessibility- or ageing-specific — [Apple Newsroom 2025-09](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/09/apples-foundation-models-framework-unlocks-new-intelligent-app-experiences/). Apple's older-adults guidance page covers configuring existing vision/hearing/cognitive settings, not an assistant — [Apple Support 126977](https://support.apple.com/en-us/126977).
- Windows: the only on-device-SLM accessibility features are Microsoft's own (Narrator, Click to Do, Fluid Dictation; see Q2). The "Text and image generation" privacy page will list third-party apps using Windows-provided generative models, implying third-party access exists, but no accessibility app using it was found — [Windows Insider blog 2025-07-18](https://blogs.windows.com/windows-insider/2025/07/18/announcing-windows-11-insider-preview-build-26200-5710-dev-channel/).
- Linux/Windows community local-model path: NVDA AI Assistant supports LiteRT-LM and llama-server fully locally — [GitHub](https://github.com/adil-adysh/NVDA-AI-assistant). NVDA core's own attempt was reverted (Q2).
- Chrome's Gemini Nano is gated at 22 GB disk / >4 GB VRAM / 16 GB RAM, which excludes most older adults' machines — [developer.chrome.com get started](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/get-started).
- HeyClicky and OpenClicky, the closest desktop "teach me where to click" products, both use cloud frontier models for the screen-understanding step (OpenClicky's on-device part is dictation only) — [heyclicky.com](https://www.heyclicky.com/); [openclicky.flowsxr.com](https://openclicky.flowsxr.com/).

### Inferences
- The field is empty at the intersection of (OS-vendor on-device model) x (desktop task guidance) x (older adults / low literacy / low vision). Prior art exists for each pair but not the triple.
- Hardware gating is the practical reason: Apple Intelligence needs M-series/A17 Pro+, Copilot+ needs a 40+ TOPS NPU, Chrome's Nano needs a discrete-class GPU. Older adults disproportionately own older hardware, so any OS-model-first design must specify a cloud or "no model" fallback.

### Gaps
- Searches may have missed App Store apps using Foundation Models for accessibility that have no web or press footprint.
- No source on Windows AI Foundry / Phi Silica third-party accessibility apps.

## Key question 4: What do these works say about which tasks a small model can be trusted with, and how to signal uncertainty to a vulnerable user?

### Takeaway
The vendor record says small on-device models are trusted with captioning, OCR, and dictation clean-up, never with autonomous action; the BLV literature finds that users rate AI descriptions trustworthy even when ~22% are wrong, that they cannot verify without sight and tend to self-blame, and that the most effective uncertainty signal tested so far is showing disagreement between multiple answers (ASSETS 2025) rather than a confidence score.

### Cited Findings
- Task scoping by vendors: Google used Nano for automatic captions and routed detail requests to server Gemini — [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2024/09/talkback-uses-gemini-nano-to-increase-low-vision-accessibility.html); Microsoft uses the local model for image description and dictation clean-up but disables Fluid Dictation in password/PIN fields — [Microsoft Support: Fluid dictation](https://support.microsoft.com/ja-jp/Accessibility/windows/voice-access/fluid-dictation); NV Access judged ViT-GPT2 captions too low quality to ship — [PR 18475](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/pull/18475).
- Accuracy vs trust: 22.2% incorrect answers and 10.8% abstentions yet trust 3.76/5 and satisfaction 4.13/5 over two weeks with 20 BLV users — [arXiv 2602.13469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13469).
- "Surfacing Variations to Calibrate Perceived Reliability of MLLM-generated Image Descriptions", Chen, Iyer, Pavel, ASSETS 2025 (peer-reviewed), arXiv 2507.15692: querying several MLLMs multiple times, splitting outputs into atomic facts and surfacing where they vary raised 15 BLV participants' ability to spot unreliable claims about 4.9x and lowered perceived reliability from 5.78/7 to 3.93/7; 14 of 15 preferred variations; three presentations including a variation summary and hierarchical descriptions for screen-reader navigation — [arXiv 2507.15692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15692v1); [a11y-paradise review](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69abb5b09a729255f81bc7d6) (secondary). The same paper notes blind users assumed auto-generated captions were correct and one described people treating AI as an authority — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3663547.3746393).
- "Explainable AI for Blind and Low-Vision Users: Navigating Trust, Modality, and Interpretability in the Agentic Era", Sakib, Dey, Zhang, Akter, CHI 2026 HCXAI workshop, arXiv 2604.00187: XAI is predominantly visual; agentic multi-step action means one undetected error propagates; BLV users strongly prefer conversational explanations and frequently self-blame for AI failures, motivating "blame-aware" explanation design — [arXiv 2604.00187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00187).
- SceneScout (street-view agent for blind users) reported moderate trust (3.5/5) with participants unwilling to rely on descriptions without physically checking — [arXiv 2504.09227](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09227).
- OLLA participants asked for orientation ("where am I, what options, what next"), confirmation before hard-to-undo actions, and a reliable completion signal; some withdrew banking/password tasks — [Nielsen UX Roundup](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ux-roundup-20260925) (secondary summary of arXiv 2609.00524).
- "Toward a Human-Centered Evaluation Framework for Trustworthy LLM-Powered GUI Agents", Chen et al. (Notre Dame et al.), arXiv 2504.17934 (position paper, Apr 2025): GUI agents process sensitive data with little oversight; proposes risk assessment, in-context consent and privacy/security built into design and evaluation — [arXiv 2504.17934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17934).
- Older adults' instructions are indirect, ambiguous and under-specified, degrading agent performance (ElderBench) — [arXiv 2609.04850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04850); LLM rephrasing of older adults' problem descriptions raised solution accuracy 35% to 69% — [arXiv 2601.10018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10018); GuideMe's clarifying-question loop lengthened asking time but cut wrong clicks to 0.11 — [a11y-paradise review](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69e7deb19cd3fefd1e4d88bd).
- Cognitive impairment: protective constraints that suit severe impairment restrict autonomy at mild impairment, so safety mechanisms must be dynamic — [arXiv 2608.17175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17175).

### Inferences
- A defensible trust boundary for a small model, consistent with what vendors ship and what studies find: describe/caption, read/OCR, rephrase/clean dictation, locate-and-point; anything that changes state (send, pay, delete, change settings) requires confirmation or a larger model, and secure fields are excluded outright.
- Uncertainty should be shown as content, not a number: a spoken "I am not sure, here are two readings" (variation surfacing) and explicit abstention ("I can't see a Save button") map onto the evidence; confidence percentages have no support in this literature for BLV or older users.
- Because BLV users self-blame, the assistant's language should attribute failure to itself ("I could not find it") rather than to the user's question.

### Gaps
- No study found that tests uncertainty signalling specifically with older adults in a desktop context.
- No paper found measuring small (sub-10B, on-device) model accuracy on screen-description tasks for BLV users; the lightweight-VLM paper (arXiv 2511.10615) was not read.

## Key question 5: Any work on "guidance instead of action" (pointing where to click, graduated autonomy) with on-device models?

### Takeaway
Guidance-over-action is now a published, peer-reviewed interaction pattern (GuideMe, CHI 2026: clarify intent, then a pointing overlay; results close to in-person help) and the commercial pattern of Clicky/HeyClicky/OpenClicky; the "beyond automation" findings from blind users (OLLA) and the design-space taxonomy (IUI 2026, Apple) support graduated control — but every instance uses a cloud model; no on-device implementation of pointing/guidance has been evaluated.

### Cited Findings
- GuideMe: formative study showed in-person helpers point at the target and eye-tracking showed focused gaze there; the system's "hollowed-out" overlay acts as a machine pointing gesture; vs in-person tutor there was no significant difference on most UEQ-S/NASA-TLX dimensions; eye-tracking revealed "inattentional blindness" (gaze passing over the right button without registering it) in unaided search; depends on well-structured Accessibility trees — [a11y-paradise review](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69e7deb19cd3fefd1e4d88bd); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791448).
- ExplorAR (arXiv 2508.01282, Aug 2025, not LLM-based): AR trial-and-error guidance for older adults, 18 participants aged 60+, highlights only the element leading to the next step — [arXiv 2508.01282](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01282). HelpMe (2017) is the earlier overlay precedent — [Duerr et al. PDF](http://www.mkhamis.com/data/papers/duerr2017olderadults.pdf).
- OLLA users' demands ("tell me where I am, what options are available, and what I should do next"; confirm before hard-to-undo; reliable done signal) are guidance requests layered on an automation agent — [Nielsen UX Roundup](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ux-roundup-20260925).
- IUI 2026 design space (Apple): user control and explainability are two of four top-level categories, validated with normal, error-prone and risky task conditions — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/mapping).
- Shipped pointing products: Clicky's `[POINT:x,y:label:screenN]` overlay — [farzaa/clicky README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/farzaa/clicky/main/README.md); HeyClicky "draws directly on your screen to show where to go" with a separate, metered "agent" mode (25/150/1,000 messages per month by tier), i.e. graduated autonomy priced as a tier — [heyclicky.com](https://www.heyclicky.com/); OpenClicky's pointer from the notch plus "simple tasks run immediately, larger jobs go to a Codex agent" — [openclicky.flowsxr.com](https://openclicky.flowsxr.com/).
- Cognitive-impairment study's "AI Capability Constraint" and "Human Oversight Embedding" mechanisms are graduated-autonomy levers by another name — [arXiv 2608.17175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17175).
- Apple's Screen Recognition is the on-device precedent for locating UI elements from pixels (CHI 2021) — [CMU news](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/news/2021/cmu-apple-team-improves-ios-app-accessibility).

### Inferences
- Pointing is cheaper than acting for a small model: it needs grounding (which element) but not planning, constraint tracking or termination, which were three of OLLA's four failure classes. That makes "point, don't click" the task class where an on-device model is most plausibly adequate, and where the accessibility-tree route (used by GuideMe, OLLA, Savant) avoids vision entirely.
- GuideMe's clarify-then-point loop and HeyClicky's teach/agent split are the two published shapes of graduated autonomy; neither has been tested with an on-device model or with desktop older adults, so a study pairing them would be first.

### Gaps
- No evaluation found of pointing/guidance driven by an on-device model (Apple Foundation Models, Phi Silica, Gemini Nano) with any population.
- No desktop (macOS/Windows) guidance study with older adults found; GuideMe, EasyAsk, ExplorAR are smartphone.
- Full text of the IUI 2026 taxonomy (specific control/confirmation findings) was not accessible.
